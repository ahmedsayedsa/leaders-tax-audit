How to Register for VAT in Egypt: Step-by-Step Guide

November 2026 · 9 min read
Quick Answer

VAT registration becomes mandatory when your taxable sales reach EGP 500,000 within any twelve months, and the application must be filed within 30 days of crossing that threshold. You register through your competent tax office or the Egyptian Tax Authority's online portal, after which you receive a nine-digit tax registration number and your monthly return and e-invoicing obligations begin.

Registering for VAT is not an administrative formality you schedule at your convenience; it is a legal obligation triggered by a specific event — your sales reaching a threshold set by law. The practical problem is that many companies discover they crossed the threshold months after the fact, register late with retroactive effect, and absorb differences and penalties that a single monitored number in their books would have avoided entirely. This guide explains how to register for VAT in Egypt as it actually happens: when the obligation starts, who must register regardless of any threshold, the steps and documents involved, and what changes in your company from the day after you register.

One methodological warning before the steps: the threshold is measured on taxable sales over any rolling twelve months — not over your financial year or the calendar year. Monitoring must therefore be continuous on a rolling basis; a single strong month can push your last-twelve-months total above the threshold even though the current year has not ended.

When does VAT registration become mandatory?

The core rule sits in Article 16 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016: any natural or legal person who sells a taxable good or supplies a taxable service must register once total taxable sales reach EGP 500,000 within twelve months. Three words carry the entire practical burden. "Taxable sales" — not all of your revenue counts, only sales of taxable goods and services, with the law's rules for mixed activities; "within twelve months" — a rolling window unconnected to the start of your financial year; and "reach" — crossing the threshold even for a single day creates the obligation; sales do not need to stay above it.

From the crossing date, the thirty-day window to file your application starts running. In practice, we advise any company whose rolling taxable sales approach EGP 400,000 to start preparing its registration file early, because discovering the crossing late — typically at a quarter-end book closing — consumes the whole window before you have even begun. To pin down exactly where you stand relative to the e-invoicing system's own threshold, see our article on the e-invoice registration threshold: the two systems are linked, though their thresholds differ.

A worked example shows why the calendar year misleads so many: a company with steady taxable sales of EGP 60,000 a month looks comfortably below the threshold if you only look at a calendar year that has just begun, yet on the rolling twelve-month basis it reaches EGP 540,000 within nine months — it crossed the threshold in its ninth month. And a seasonal business may cross in one strong quarter and then fall back, yet its registration obligation stands, because the triggering event — the crossing — has already occurred. Compute the figure from your books, on taxable sales only: sales the law exempts do not enter the calculation, and categories with special rules, such as schedule goods and exports, are treated under the rules the law sets for each. One extra column in your monthly book closing — the trailing twelve-month total — is enough to catch the crossing in time.

Who must register even below the threshold?

The EGP 500,000 figure is not the only gateway into registration. The law requires certain categories to register regardless of the threshold because of the nature of their activity. Examples prescribed by law include:

  • Importers who import taxable goods or services for trading purposes.
  • Exporters who export goods or services and need registration to recover input VAT.
  • Those dealing in schedule goods and services — items subject to schedule tax under special rates or rules.
  • Agents and distributors acting for their principals in the cases the law prescribes.

These are examples of cases the law sets out; the detailed list and each case's conditions must be checked against the governing texts and instructions before concluding that a given activity is covered. The working rule: if your activity touches importing, exporting, or schedule goods, do not assume that small sales exempt you from registration — verify first. And if you buy from suppliers and want to confirm their tax standing, our article on how to verify a VAT registration explains how to check any counterparty's registration number.

What are the steps to register for VAT?

The registration procedure itself is straightforward; most of its time is spent preparing documents, not filing the application. The steps in their practical order:

  1. Establish your position precisely: compute your taxable sales over the last twelve months and determine whether your registration is mandatory for crossing the threshold, mandatory by the nature of the activity, or voluntary.
  2. Prepare the document file listed in the next section, and make sure the data matches across the commercial register, the tax card, and the company contract; a mismatched address or activity between documents is one of the most common causes of delay.
  3. File the registration application with the tax office competent for your activity, or through the Egyptian Tax Authority's online portal at eta.gov.eg via the channels available to your activity, and supply any further data or documents the office requests.
  4. Collect your registration certificate and tax registration number — nine digits — and ensure it is printed on your tax invoices from the first invoice you issue after registration.
  5. Activate your post-registration duties immediately: the monthly return, sound invoicing, and linkage to the e-invoicing system if your activity falls within its phases — our e-invoicing service covers this step end to end.

Our professional opinion on this point is blunt: do not file the application before your invoicing system is ready to issue correct tax invoices from day one. Registration opens the door to deducting input VAT, but it also turns every invoice you issue into an auditable tax document; the gap between your registration date and your system's readiness date is the single most common source of differences at a first audit.

Which documents are required?

The documents usually required for the registration file — noting that the tax office may request more depending on legal form and activity — are:

  • A recently issued commercial register extract for the establishment or company.
  • The tax card with current data.
  • The company contract and articles of association, with any amendments.
  • Proof of the activity's premises: a notarized lease or ownership deed for the head office and any branches.
  • National ID cards of the partners or the authorized signatory, plus the power of attorney or delegation decision when an agent files the application.
  • Details of activities, branches, and storage locations, if any.

For sole establishments and liberal professions the set differs slightly, but the logic is constant: a document proving the entity, a document proving the premises, and a document proving the applicant's authority. Preparing these papers before you need them is implicitly part of a sound company formation service; a company incorporated with a clean file from day one never stalls at a later registration.

Practical Warning

The thirty days run from the day you cross the threshold, not the day you notice it. If you find your sales passed the threshold months ago, the right move is to register immediately and handle the earlier period with sound technical treatment — never to delay further; additional delay never improves the position.

Can you register voluntarily before reaching the threshold?

Yes. The law permits voluntary registration for those below the threshold who meet its conditions, and it is a decision worth thinking through rather than dismissing out of hand. Its practical justifications:

  • Input VAT deduction: as a registrant you deduct the tax charged on your purchases and inputs from the tax due on your sales — which can change your activity's economics if your taxable purchases are large.
  • Dealing with large companies: many registered companies explicitly or implicitly require registered suppliers, because invoices from the unregistered give them no sound deduction — registration here is your entry ticket to bigger clients.
  • Readiness for growth: if your sales are already approaching the threshold, orderly early registration beats a rushed filing under deadline pressure.

Against that, voluntary registration means a monthly commitment to returns and tax invoicing and full visibility within the system; if your business is small and your clients are individuals rather than companies, the administrative cost may exceed the benefit. The right decision is built on the structure of your clients and purchases, not on impression — exactly the kind of question we answer within our tax and tax audit service.

What happens after registration — and which mistakes must you avoid?

From the day you register, your relationship with the tax changes fundamentally. You become a collection agent for the state: you charge the standard 14% on your taxable sales, deduct the tax you paid on your inputs, and remit the difference to the Authority under a monthly return. Your invoices become tax invoices bearing your registration number, and your books become the basis of a position that will one day be audited. Registration also connects you to the e-invoicing system, which verifies invoices in real time, so any flaw in your registration or data shows up immediately in your dealings with registered clients.

Three post-registration mistakes recur in the audits we see:

  • Missing the thirty-day deadline in the first place, then trying to ignore the gap between crossing the threshold and registering — a gap discoverable from system data and records.
  • Registering without an invoicing system in place, so invoices go out with missing data or no registration number, weakening your clients' deductions and bouncing the problem back to you commercially and fiscally.
  • Neglecting the nil return in months with no activity; a registrant must file the monthly return even with no transactions, and accumulated unfiled returns generate assessments and penalties unrelated to your real activity.

What about branches and multiple activities?

Registration covers the establishment or company as one entity, including its activities and branches within its tax file. If your company runs more than one activity — some taxable, some exempt — or operates from several premises and branches in different governorates, the registration file must reflect that full picture from the start: every activity declared in the commercial register, every premises, branch and storage location, with the tax office notified of additions or changes under the prescribed procedures. The costliest error here is registering under a single activity while actually practising another, or opening a new branch that starts selling before it is added to the file; sales and invoices then appear with no counterpart in the registration data, and a simple data gap turns into a full audit subject. The rule: your registration file must match your company's reality on any day it is opened, not only on registration day.

The conclusion we work by with our clients: VAT registration is a one-day step, but managing it correctly is a monthly system.

Whoever builds it in an orderly way from the start — tidy books, sound invoicing, monitored thresholds and deadlines — turns VAT into a tool for deducting inputs and dealing with major clients; whoever leaves it to improvisation turns it into a source of audit differences. If you are approaching the threshold or already past it, start by reviewing your position before the tax office starts reviewing you.
