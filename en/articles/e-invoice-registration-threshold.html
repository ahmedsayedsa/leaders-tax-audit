Egypt E-Invoice Registration Threshold: EGP 250K or 500K?

November 2026 · 8 min read
Quick Answer

The mandatory VAT registration threshold in Egypt is EGP 500,000 of sales within twelve months, under Article 16 of Law No. 67 of 2016 — and it has not changed. The EGP 250,000 figure circulating online is a misreading of Minister of Finance Decision 281 of 2025, which concerns the e-receipt system for specifically named categories, effective 15 September 2025, and has nothing to do with the VAT registration threshold.

"I heard the registration threshold dropped to 250K — is that true?" This is one of the most frequent questions we have received in recent months, and it originates in circulating posts that connect two figures and two decisions that have nothing to do with each other. The danger is not the wrong number itself, but the decisions built on it: companies that registered for VAT prematurely for no reason, and — more dangerously — companies that reassured themselves they were "below the new threshold" and delayed obligations that were actually already in force. This guide untangles the confusion from its official sources: what the real registration threshold is, where the 250K figure came from, and the distinction that must stay clear between the e-invoice and the e-receipt.

What is the mandatory VAT registration threshold?

The mandatory VAT registration threshold is EGP 500,000: anyone who sells a taxable good or supplies a taxable service and whose total sales reach this amount within twelve months must register for the tax. This is the text of Article 16 of the Value Added Tax Law No. 67 of 2016, and it is what the Egyptian Tax Authority itself confirms in the FAQ section of its official website. No legislative amendment lowering this threshold has been issued to date.

The table below summarises the three figures the confusion revolves around, and what each one really means:

Figure or dateWhat it really meansIts source
EGP 500,000The mandatory VAT registration threshold — sales within 12 monthsArticle 16, Law 67 of 2016
EGP 250,000A circulating figure that is not a registration threshold; it spread through a misreading of an e-receipt decisionDecision 281 of 2025 (in an entirely different context)
15 September 2025The start of the obligation for named categories listed in an annex to issue electronic receiptsThe same Decision 281 of 2025

Note also that crossing the registration threshold is not the only route to a VAT registration duty; there are cases where registration is required regardless of this threshold, depending on the nature of the activity and transactions. The practical rule, therefore, is not "my sales are under EGP 500K, so I am outside every system", but rather to review your position under each obligation separately — which is what the following sections of this guide do.

Where did the EGP 250,000 figure come from?

The story began with Minister of Finance Decision No. 281 of 2025. The actual decision — as published and as explained by the Tax Authority — concerns the e-receipt system, specifically the second sub-phase of the eighth main phase of that system's rollout. Under it, taxpayers whose names appear in a list attached to the decision must issue electronic receipts starting 15 September 2025. The decision, in other words, is about: named categories, a receipts system, and a specific start date.

The 250K figure slipped into circulation when some readers misread the decision and linked it to the VAT registration threshold; posts then passed the number along as "the new threshold" until it came to look like settled fact. The truth, as announced by Rasha Abdel Aal, Head of the Egyptian Tax Authority: the decision does not change the VAT registration threshold, and the threshold remains EGP 500,000 as set in the law.

Our professional view of this episode goes beyond correcting a number: every tax decision should be read from its official source, or through a specialist who reads the full decision with its lists and phases, because an abbreviated post can turn the meaning upside down — exactly as happened here, when a receipts decision for specified categories became a "general cut to the registration threshold" in the minds of thousands of followers.

A Rule That Protects You From This Confusion

When a new tax figure reaches you through social media, ask three things before believing it: the number of the law or decision, which system it concerns (VAT? e-invoice? e-receipt?), and which categories it applies to. If any of the three is missing, the figure is incomplete and no decision should be built on it.

What is the difference between the e-invoice and the e-receipt?

Half the confusion in this topic comes from mixing up two different systems with similar names. The e-invoice is the document issued in business-to-business dealings (B2B): a supplier selling to a company, or a service provider dealing with an establishment. It is the document on which cost substantiation and input VAT deduction between companies are built, and its mandate was issued in successive phases through decisions specifying the obliged categories in each phase.

The e-receipt, by contrast, is the document issued when selling to the final consumer (B2C): a shop selling to an individual customer, or a service provider dealing with the public directly. Its system is independent, and so are its mandate phases and decisions — including Decision 281 of 2025, which started this whole confusion. A single company may need both systems if it sells to businesses and to the public at the same time.

The relationship between the two systems and VAT registration must stay equally clear: registration for the tax is one obligation with its own statutory threshold (EGP 500,000), and joining each system is a separate obligation with its own decisions, categories, and dates. Three separate obligations — not one obligation with three names. Our e-invoice and e-receipt service covers both systems together for precisely this reason.

Is the e-invoice mandate tied to a sales threshold at all?

No — not in the way many imagine. The e-invoice mandate was issued in phases, through decisions specifying the obliged categories in each phase — by the nature of the activity, the legal form, and the category — not through a single rule saying "whoever's sales exceed a certain amount joins". That is why you can find two companies with similar sales volumes where one has been obliged to join the system for some time and the other entered in a later phase: the mandate criterion was category and phase, not the sales figure alone.

The important practical consequence: do not use the EGP 500,000 threshold to judge your e-invoice position. The threshold settles exactly one question — must you register for VAT? The e-invoice question is settled by the phase decisions and the Authority's lists. And whoever delayed joining despite being obliged exposes himself to what we detailed in our guide on the penalty for not registering in the e-invoice system, from the Article 71 fine to the non-acceptance of paper invoices.

Nor does being "small" keep you away from every system obligation: e-receipt decisions — such as Decision 281 — address specified categories by name and nature, and some of them are relatively small retail activities that nevertheless deal with the final consumer directly. Size alone is no shield; the nature of the dealing is the criterion in the receipts system.

How do you pin down your company's exact position?

An exact position is not learned from a post or a general question, but from three ordered reviews:

  1. Your VAT position: Total your taxable sales over the last twelve months and compare them with the EGP 500,000 threshold; if you have crossed it, you must register for the tax — and if you are not registered, start with how to register for VAT, and confirm your current status through the steps for verifying VAT registration.
  2. Your e-invoice position: Review the phase decisions issued by the Tax Authority and the lists of obliged categories, and determine whether your activity and company type fall within any phase in force, and from what date.
  3. Your e-receipt position: If you sell to the final consumer, review the e-receipt system's decisions and their attached lists — including the list under Decision 281 of 2025 — to learn whether your establishment or category is covered, and when your obligation begins.

After the three reviews, document the result in writing in your company's tax file: which obligations apply to you, from which dates, what has been done about them, and what remains. This documentation is what protects you at any future audit, and it is the first thing we request from any new client in our tax and tax audit service before starting any procedure.

What should you do if you have crossed the threshold?

If the review shows your sales crossed EGP 500,000 within twelve months and you are not registered for VAT, what is required is immediate action, not watchful waiting: register for the tax first, then arrange joining the e-invoice system (and the e-receipt system if you sell to the public), then review with your chartered accountant the period in which you operated above the threshold without registration, to assess its position and remediate it in a documented way.

One final point of no less importance: some companies below the threshold ask about voluntary VAT registration before reaching it, for practical reasons such as dealing with large companies that require tax invoices from their suppliers. That decision has advantages and obligations together — periodic returns and continuing responsibilities — so do not take it based on general advice; discuss it with your accountant in light of your actual clients and the nature of your market.

The conclusion we want to stay with you: EGP 500K is the VAT threshold and it has not changed; EGP 250K is a phantom figure in this context; and Decision 281 concerns e-receipts for named categories from 15 September 2025. Three sentences that untangle the whole confusion — and any content that mixes them up does not deserve to have a tax decision built on it.
